Diabetes and Heart Health — Protecting Your Heart

Why diabetes raises heart risk, and how food, activity, and habits protect your heart.

Why heart health matters

People with diabetes have a higher risk of heart disease and stroke than people without diabetes, partly because high blood sugar damages blood vessels over time, and partly because diabetes often goes with high blood pressure, high cholesterol, and excess weight. Heart disease is the most common serious complication of diabetes, so protecting your heart is a central part of living well with the condition. The good news is that the same habits that help blood sugar also help the heart, so the advice is consistent, not a set of competing rules.

Fats: the type matters

The type of fat you eat matters more than the total amount. Saturated fat, found in fatty meat, butter, cheese, and many processed foods, raises LDL cholesterol, the harmful kind, and should be limited. Unsaturated fats, found in olive oil, rapeseed oil, nuts, seeds, and oily fish, help lower LDL cholesterol and support heart health. Replace butter with olive oil, choose lean meat and remove visible fat, and eat oily fish such as salmon, mackerel, or sardines twice a week. Avoid trans fats, found in some fried and baked processed foods, which are the most harmful fats of all.

Salt and blood pressure

High blood pressure is common with diabetes and damages blood vessels, so keeping salt moderate matters. Most salt comes from processed foods, bread, cheese, and sauces, not from the salt you add at the table. Check labels and choose lower-salt options where you can, cook with herbs, spices, garlic, and lemon instead of salt, and limit processed meats, ready meals, and salty snacks. Aim for less than 6 grams of salt a day, which is about a teaspoon. Reducing salt gradually lets your taste adjust without food tasting bland.

Fibre, fruit, and vegetables

Fibre helps lower cholesterol as well as blood sugar, so the high-fibre foods that help diabetes also help the heart: oats, barley, beans, lentils, fruit, and vegetables. Eat at least five portions of fruit and vegetables a day, with a variety of colours. Whole grains are better than refined ones for both blood sugar and cholesterol. Nuts and seeds, in small portions, provide healthy fats and fibre. These foods form the base of the Mediterranean pattern, which has the strongest evidence for heart health in diabetes.

Activity, weight, and smoking

Regular activity strengthens the heart, lowers blood pressure, and improves cholesterol. Aim for 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, plus strength training twice a week. Losing even a modest amount of weight improves blood pressure and cholesterol. If you smoke, stopping is the single most powerful thing you can do for your heart; ask your care team for support, because quitting is hard and help makes it much more likely to succeed. Limit alcohol, which raises blood pressure and adds calories.

Working with your care team

Your care team will check your blood pressure and cholesterol regularly and may prescribe medicine to protect your heart, such as a statin or blood pressure tablets. Take these as prescribed, even if you feel well, because they prevent problems rather than treating symptoms. Bring your food and activity notes to appointments, and discuss your heart risk and targets with your team. Protecting your heart is a long-term project, and the daily habits of eating well, moving, and not smoking add up to a large difference over years.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does diabetes increase heart risk?

High blood sugar damages blood vessels over time, and diabetes often goes with high blood pressure, high cholesterol, and excess weight. Together these raise the risk of heart disease and stroke.

What fats are best for heart health with diabetes?

Unsaturated fats from olive oil, nuts, seeds, and oily fish. Limit saturated fat from fatty meat, butter, and cheese, and avoid trans fats in processed foods.

How much salt should I eat?

Less than 6 grams a day, about a teaspoon. Most salt comes from processed foods, so check labels and cook with herbs and spices instead.
